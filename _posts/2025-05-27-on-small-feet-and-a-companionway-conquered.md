I have watched a great many things go wrong on a great many boats, and
I generally consider myself hard to surprise. Eli surprised me today.
Not yet one year old, not walking properly on land as far as I've
seen, and he hauled himself up the companionway steps today, one small
determined hand over the other, because Jo went up first to find Tobi
at the helm, and Eli, watching from the cabin sole, apparently decided
that whatever his big brother could do, he could do too.

Nobody taught him that climb, not directly. I was belowdecks and saw
the whole thing: Jo scrambling up without a second thought, the way he
always does, and Eli right behind him a few moments later, copying
each handhold as best a smaller set of arms could manage. No
hesitation, no crying for help, just a baby who watched his brother
solve four steep wooden steps and decided the solution would work for
him too. He got to the top, found Tobi at the tiller, and grinned like
he'd just crossed an ocean. In a sense he had.

I have minded the comings and goings of a great many crews over a great
many centuries, and I will say this plainly: I did not expect a
one-year-old to out-climb half the able seamen I've known. Land-walking
can apparently wait. A boat teaches its own priorities first.
