A calm, unremarkable afternoon hop brought us into Sant Antoni in
broad daylight, a welcome change after the long, dark, weakening-wind
crossing to Cala Portinatx the night before. We were barely made fast
to the pontoon before Melusina One pulled in behind us and rafted up
practically on top of our own stern cleat. The only Luxembourg-flagged
boat this crew has ever met, twice now, the second time by nothing but
sheer bad odds.

I do not believe in coincidence, professionally speaking. A spirit who
has spent centuries reading currents and crossings learns that the sea
is smaller than it looks and considerably more repetitive than sailors
like to admit. Boats on similar routes, at similar speeds, chasing
similar weather windows, tend to keep bumping into one another whether
either crew intends it or not. That is not fate. That is just how
currents and calendars work.

Still, I will allow that it was a pleasant surprise, and that Vicky and
Laurent are good company for a crew that has been mostly talking to
itself and one moody helmsman for the past several days. The marinero
here, a fellow named Leo, deserves a mention too, for getting everyone
sorted without incident, though this time in broad daylight rather
than by torchlight. Sant Antoni itself I will reserve judgment on. A
town built entirely around the young and the loud is not my usual
haunt, but then again, neither was any port until someone built one
there.
