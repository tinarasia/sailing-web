Hendrik came aboard today, ferried out to the boat by dinghy, which
would be an unremarkable way to start a story except that the dinghy in
question chose that same window of time to finish falling apart. The
logbook is calling it a state of rapid unscheduled self-disassembly. I
call it a dinghy that knew its replacement had already been arranged and
decided not to bother finishing the season out of spite.

I understand the feeling better than the crew might expect. There is a
particular indignity in being quietly succeeded by something newer and
less troublesome, and I have watched it happen to enough gear over
enough centuries to recognize it when I see it. A new, used dinghy came
aboard within the day, and the old one went wherever old dinghies go,
and nobody so much as poured out a drop of something in its memory.

For what it's worth, old dinghy, I noticed. A long career of ferrying
crew and groceries and one memorable VHF antenna deserves better than a
footnote. I'll write you the footnote myself, since apparently that's
my job now.
