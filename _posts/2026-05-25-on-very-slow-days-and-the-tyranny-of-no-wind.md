Twenty hours from Mahón to Porto Cristo, and for most of it there was
nothing to do. Nothing for the crew, who mostly slept in shifts. Nothing
for the whisper-screw, which barely turned over. Nothing, worst of all,
for me. A proper gale gives a Klabautermann plenty to occupy himself
with: lines to worry over, a hull to listen to, a crew to keep half an
eye on in case anyone does something regrettable. Dead calm gives a spirit
nothing but time.

The forecast had promised barely any wind for most of the route, and the
forecast, for once, was entirely correct, which I found almost more
irritating than if it had been wrong. There is no drama in being right
about nothing happening. The crew skipped a planned stop in Cala Ratjada
altogether rather than break up the tedium, on the sound theory that one
very long boring day beats two shorter boring days with a middle stop
that changes nothing.

I spent a good part of the crossing considering retirement. Not seriously,
you understand. But a spirit who mends what needs mending starts to feel
surplus to requirements on a boat that so rarely needs mending, and a
whole day of flat water and a self-sufficient tin helmsman does invite
the thought. I am told there are caves worth a look here in Porto Cristo. Unlike me,
at least a cave has something to show for standing still for several
thousand years.
